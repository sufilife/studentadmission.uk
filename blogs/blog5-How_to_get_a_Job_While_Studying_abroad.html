How to Get a Job While Studying Abroad

  1. Home
  2. Blogs
  3. How to Get a Job While Studying Abroad

Working while studying abroad is one of the most effective ways for international students to gain experience, cover expenses, and build confidence. Many students find part-time jobs and internships that help them grow professionally while balancing academic life.

Depending on your visa type and country, you might have legal permission to work a limited number of hours per week. Understanding your rights, preparing a strong CV, and knowing where to look can significantly increase your chances of finding a job abroad.

Check Your Work Rights First

Each country has different rules for international students:

  • UK: Up to 20 hours/week during term time.
  • Canada: Off-campus work allowed with restrictions.
  • Australia: Up to 48 hours per fortnight.
  • USA: Mostly on-campus unless CPT/OPT is approved.

Always check the exact conditions listed on your visa or BRP card before applying.

Where to Find Jobs Abroad

Some of the most reliable job search places for international students include:

  • University career portals
  • On-campus positions such as library or administrative roles
  • Online job portals like Indeed, Reed, LinkedIn, SEEK
  • Local coffee shops, restaurants, and supermarkets
  • Student job fairs and networking events

Create a Strong CV

A clean, professional CV can significantly increase your chances. Highlight your strengths, language skills, volunteer experience, and education. Tailor your CV based on each job you apply for.

Develop Skills Employers Value

Even if you have no job experience, you can stand out by developing in-demand skills like:

  • Customer Service
  • Basic Computer Skills
  • Teamwork & Communication
  • Time Management
  • Multitasking

Common Student Job Options Abroad

  • Retail Associate
  • Restaurant Staff / Barista
  • Teaching Assistant
  • Warehouse helper (country rules apply)
  • Internships in your study field

Stay Consistent

Finding your first job may take some time. Stay consistent, apply regularly, improve your CV, and keep learning. Once you secure your first job abroad, everything becomes easier.
